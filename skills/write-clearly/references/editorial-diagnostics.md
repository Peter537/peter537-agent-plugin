# Editorial diagnostics and rewrite strategies

Diagnose the underlying communication defect rather than searching for forbidden words or punctuation.

## Trust and content defects

- **Unsupported importance:** claims that a change is transformative, essential, or industry-leading without evidence. Replace with the concrete capability or remove the evaluation.
- **Invented specificity:** plausible metrics, examples, citations, names, or causes added to make vague prose sound concrete. Research or omit; never fabricate.
- **False certainty:** uncertainty, proposal, opinion, or partial evidence rewritten as established fact. Restore the correct evidentiary status.
- **Vague attribution:** unnamed experts, teams, or studies used as authority. Preserve the actual attribution or remove the unsupported appeal.
- **Shallow interpretation:** a factual sentence followed by generic commentary that adds no decision-relevant meaning. Keep the fact or add evidenced analysis.

## Structure and information defects

- **Buried purpose:** history or setup appears before the reader's goal, result, or decision. Move the needed outcome earlier when the document function permits.
- **Mixed functions:** tutorial, procedure, reference, explanation, migration, and release chronology compete in one section. Separate or clearly signpost them.
- **Historical accumulation:** obsolete implementation states remain in current guidance. Preserve decision rationale, migration instructions, or release history in their proper home; do not delete unique history blindly.
- **Duplicate framing:** several sections repeat the same background instead of linking to one authority. Keep one canonical explanation.
- **Implementation-shaped navigation:** headings mirror internal components when readers need task or decision paths. Reorganize only when structural authority exists.

## Sentence and paragraph defects

- **Hidden action:** nominalizations or indirect phrases obscure a simple verb and responsible actor. Name the action and actor when they matter.
- **Qualifier stack:** nested caveats make scope hard to follow. Reorder conditions, split propositions, or define the boundary once without dropping it.
- **Terminology drift:** synonyms imply distinctions or make translation harder. Use one term per concept unless the distinction is intentional.
- **Template rhythm:** paragraphs or sentences repeat the same shape without helping navigation. Vary structure only as needed for comprehension.
- **Empty transition:** connective language announces movement without clarifying the relationship. State the relationship or remove the transition.
- **Overloaded sentence:** independent conditions, actors, or outcomes are packed together. Split at a logical boundary, not an arbitrary length.

## Rhetorical defects

- **Manufactured contrast:** a “not X but Y” construction exaggerates a distinction or repeats the same claim. State the actual distinction directly.
- **Decorative climax:** forced triples, fragments, or aphoristic endings substitute rhythm for information. Keep them only when the chosen voice and function benefit.
- **Inflated metaphor:** figurative language obscures technical meaning or excludes global readers. Replace it with literal language unless the analogy genuinely teaches.
- **Generic optimism:** the ending predicts a promising future or broad impact without evidence. End on the decision, action, limitation, or concrete result.
- **Promotional neutrality breach:** reference, policy, or safety prose contains sales language. Move approved persuasion to an appropriate surface.

## Model and workflow residue

Remove acknowledgments, restatements of the request, process narration, generic courtesy sign-offs, unrequested alternative versions, placeholders, and tool residue when they are not part of the intended document. Do not label them as proof of machine authorship.

## Formatting defects

Use headings, lists, tables, and emphasis for reader tasks rather than decoration. Prefer a numbered list for a true sequence, bullets for parallel items, and prose when list labels merely fragment a coherent thought. Preserve valid syntax, anchors, and rendering behavior.

## Contextual heuristics

Em dashes, semicolons, contractions, passive voice, fragments, first person, repetition, humor, adjectives, adverbs, questions, analogies, and long sentences are not defects by themselves. Evaluate whether each choice supports the document function, audience, language, author, and controlling style.
